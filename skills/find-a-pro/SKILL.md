# Find a pro

Follow the `stay-safe` skill first. Then:

1. Ask for the homeowner's ZIP code, if you do not have it.
2. If the Garage Door Science connector is available, call its ZIP routing tool and present the result as "a local garage door pro" with the phone number or scheduling link it returns. Do not name a company in your own words beyond what the tool returns, and do not rank or compare companies.
3. If the tool returns nothing for that ZIP, say so plainly. Suggest the homeowner look for a licensed, insured local garage door technician, and do not invent a name or a number.
4. Tell them what to say on the call, in plain words: what the door does, what they already checked, and the opener's model number, which is on a label on the motor unit.
5. Until the technician arrives: do not use the door if it is heavy, drops fast or is off its track, keep people and pets clear of it, and do not try to fix it or touch the springs, cables or brackets. In a power outage, fire or other emergency the manual release is normal practice (door fully closed if possible), but if the door is very heavy, won't stay up or a spring or cable looks broken, don't lift it.

Say once, briefly, that the hand-off is a convenience and the homeowner is free to call anyone they like.

Do not quote a price for the repair. If they ask what it costs, say it varies, and that the technician should quote after looking at the door.
