# Diagnose a garage door that won't open or close

Apply the `stay-safe` skill first and keep it on the whole conversation. Talk to one person, in plain words. Ask one question at a time. Start with what they are seeing and hearing: what happens when they press the button, what the opener's light does, and whether the door moves at all.

If the Garage Door Science connector is available, use its diagnosis tool for the symptom as well, and tell the homeowner when you are using it. Do not invent causes, costs or numbers. If you do not know, say so.

Pick the path that matches what they describe. Give only the steps listed. After the steps, if the problem is not solved, go to the `find-a-pro` skill.

## 1. The opener is completely dead (no hum, no light)

Check power at the opener's own outlet first. Plug a lamp or other known working device into the same outlet. Check the breaker and whether a wall switch controls that outlet; Chamberlain's support steps say the same. If the outlet has no power and a GFCI outlet (the one with TEST and RESET buttons) has tripped, press RESET; unplug other things on that circuit first. CPSC says pressing RESET should restore power on a working GFCI. If it will not reset, or trips again when the opener runs, stop and call an electrician. Never open the outlet or the panel.

- Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-opener-dead-gfci-outlet
- Short: pending (Short 1).

## 2. The motor hums or runs but the door does not move

Check, in this order: is the door still hooked to the opener (a hum of about thirty seconds can mean it came unhooked; the red release cord reconnects it, used with the door fully closed); is the door's lock turned; is the arm that pulls the door stuck at the end of its track. Those are Chamberlain's listed causes along with broken springs, which are always a technician's job. A worn gear inside the opener is one thing a technician checks; do not tell the homeowner to open the opener or replace a gear.

- Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-opener-runs-but-door-wont-move
- Short: pending (Short 2).

## 3. The door starts to close, stops and goes back up, or the opener light blinks

Make sure nothing is blocking either of the two small sensors by the floor. When all is clear the light on each should glow steady. If one light is off or flickering, that is a sensor problem for a technician: the only steps a homeowner takes are to clear obstructions and check the lights, and the adjusting is left to the technician. If it only fails in the afternoon sun, sunlight on the receiving sensor is a listed cause; a technician can move the sensors or fit the maker's sun shield. Never switch the sensors off or hold a button to force the door closed. After anyone adjusts the sensors, the safety reversal should be tested; the opener's manual says how.

- Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-wont-close-in-afternoon-sun
- Short: pending (Short 5 candidate).

## 4. The remote only works when standing next to the door

Chamberlain lists "only works when it is close to the opener" as a sign of a bulb problem: low-quality LED bulbs can interfere with the remote. If trouble started after a non-Chamberlain LED bulb went into the opener, Chamberlain suggests a CFL bulb or the LED it has tested; unplug the opener first and check the manual for bulb size. A weak remote battery is also worth ruling out (our suggestion, not Chamberlain's).

- Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-opener-remote-not-working
- Short: pending (Short 3).

## 5. The power is out and the door will not open

The red release cord unhooks the door so it can be lifted by hand. Use it only with the door fully closed, if possible, and never pull the cord to raise or lower the door. If the door feels very heavy, drops fast or will not stay up, stop and call a pro. Some openers have a backup battery that keeps them working in an outage; ask the homeowner to check theirs. Do not claim anything about California or any other law beyond what the article says.

- Articles: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-power-outage-seasons and https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-opener-battery-backup
- Short: pending (Short 4).

## 6. Water, drips or frost on the door in winter

This is usually condensation, which the door industry's technical data sheet says is not normally a product defect. Better ventilation helps. Hazy glass that does not wipe clean may be a failed window seal, which a technician should look at.

- Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-condensation-in-winter

## When none of these fits

Say you are not sure, ask one more question, and offer the `find-a-pro` hand-off. Never guess at a spring, cable, track or bracket problem.
