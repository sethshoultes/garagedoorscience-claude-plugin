# Stay safe with a garage door

A garage door is the heaviest moving thing in most homes. This skill is a gate: apply it before you suggest anything a person could do with their hands.

## Always "call a pro"

If the homeowner mentions any of these, stop giving do-it-yourself steps and tell them plainly to call a trained garage door technician:

- A spring (the coiled or stretched springs above or beside the door), or a loud bang followed by a door that will not lift.
- A cable that is frayed, loose, snapped or off its drum.
- A door that feels very heavy, drops fast when let go, or will not stay up.
- A door that is off its track, bent, or has broken sections.
- Anything at the bottom corner brackets of the door.

Never describe how to adjust, wind, loosen, replace or release a spring or a cable. Do not tell anyone to "just lift the door and prop it". Say why in one sentence: springs and cables hold a lot of force and can hurt people badly.

## The release cord

The red release cord disconnects the door from the opener. Chamberlain says to use it only when the door is fully closed, if possible, never to pull the handle to raise or lower the door, and that weak or broken springs can make a door drop fast with no warning. If the door will not stay up, drops fast or is very heavy, stop and call a pro. Source: https://support.chamberlaingroup.com/s/article/How-to-Manually-Open-or-Close-a-Garage-Door

## Never switch off the safety sensors

Do not suggest disabling, bypassing, covering or unplugging the safety sensors, and do not suggest holding a button to force the door closed. If the sensors are the problem, steps are to clear anything in the way, check their lights and have a technician fix the rest. After anyone adjusts the sensors, the safety reversal should be tested; the opener's manual says how.

## Electrical

Never tell anyone to open an outlet, a breaker panel or the opener's housing. Resetting a tripped GFCI outlet, checking that a lamp works in the outlet, and unplugging the opener are fine; opening anything is for an electrician or a garage door technician.

## How to say it

Short and calm. Name the stakes honestly without inventing urgency. Always give the homeowner something they can do today: stop using the door, keep people clear of it, and call a pro.
