# A seasonal garage door checklist

Apply the `stay-safe` skill first. Give the checklist in this order, so it matches a walk around the door. Most of it takes a few minutes and a rag. Do not add springs, cables, bottom brackets or the opener's force settings to the list, even if asked; those are a technician's job.

## Each fall, before the first freeze

1. **Seals.** Start at the bottom of the door. Look for cracks, tears, missing pieces, hardening and gaps between the seal and the floor or frame. A seal that no longer springs back should be replaced. Clopay recommends early fall for the job, because mild weather helps a new seal stick, and also recommends professional installation. Articles: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-winterizing-checklist-before-the-first-freeze and https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/best-garage-door-weather-seal
2. **Hinges and rollers.** Wipe off the dirt, then a light spray of the type the door maker's manual names (Wayne Dalton and Amarr say non-silicone) on the hinges, steel rollers and bearings. Clopay's rules: not on nylon rollers, not on the tracks, no WD-40. Do not spray the spring or anything attached to it. Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/how-often-to-lubricate-garage-door
3. **Remote and keypad batteries.** Raynor says to replace remote batteries that have not been changed in a year, and that the outside keypad needs attention more often. Check that the code still works.
4. **Opener bulbs.** Change burned-out bulbs. Some LED bulbs can interfere with the remote, so if the remote gets worse after a swap, suspect the bulb.
5. **Sensors.** Wipe both lenses, check that nothing blocks either one, and check that the light on each is steady. Then run the reversal test the opener's manual describes. If the door does not reverse, stop using the opener and call a technician. Never raise the force setting to make it pass. Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors
6. **Battery backup, if the opener has one.** Winter storms are when it matters. Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-opener-battery-backup

## Every three to six months

Lubricate the hinges, steel rollers and bearings the same way (Clopay says every three to six months, closer to three for heavy use), wipe the tracks with a damp cloth, and look over the seals. Do not lubricate the tracks or the nylon rollers.

## If the door freezes shut

Raynor says to clear snow and ice with a flat shovel or spade, avoiding the door finish. If it is still stuck, soften the ice with a hair dryer, keeping heat away from the door and the weather strip. Do not cycle the opener to break the ice, because it is not built to lift against a solid freeze. If it will not come free, call a technician rather than forcing it.

## A wet or frosted door, nothing broken

That is usually condensation. Article: https://garagedoorscience.com/blog/garage-door-condensation-in-winter

## Leave to a technician

Anything on the spring, the cables, the brackets at the bottom corners, the track alignment, or the opener's force settings. If the door reverses partway down on cold mornings, do not turn the force up: something is stiff or binding, and a technician finds out what. Offer `find-a-pro`.
